\begin{abstract}
We re-test Q-MAML~\citep{leeqmaml2025} (AAAI 2025), a meta-learned PQC initializer, on HEP problems it was
never evaluated on. First, we audit the ML4Sci GSoC 2025 codebase that applies it to HEP classification and
find both attempts broken: in one, the training loss is detached from the computation graph before
backpropagation, so the learner network never actually updates; the other runs a first-order-MAML-style
inner loop, not the paper's published algorithm. We build a paper-faithful re-implementation and calibrate
it against the paper's own VQE settings at its published 2000-iteration budget (table below).
Applied to HIGGS classification, a novel setting for this algorithm, it gets the best validation accuracy at
every qubit count tested, significant vs.\ Gaussian init and the GSoC baseline ($p<0.05$, $n=4$ seeds). A
McClean-et-al.\ barren-plateau scan shows no scheme, including ours, changes the variance-scaling exponent;
classification cost functions plateau $\sim$8 orders of magnitude faster than the VQE costs the paper was
validated on. Finally we run ten genuine lattice/field-theory (and adjacent) Hamiltonian pilots (Schwinger
model, $\mathbb{Z}_2$ lattice gauge theory, $\phi^4$ scalar field, SU(2) lattice gauge theory, collective
neutrino oscillations, supersymmetric quantum mechanics, non-Abelian $\mathbb{D}_8$ lattice gauge theory,
light nuclei in lattice pionless EFT, scalar Yukawa coupling, and the Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick model): clear
wins on six, ties for best on two (every scheme reaches the same final answer, Q-MAML only faster or
tied-fastest), a genuine null result on one (too small a system to differentiate any scheme), and one
diagnosed loss (gauge invariance enforced by a Gauss-law penalty), plus several corrections to claims made
earlier in the paper. A second classification dataset (quark-gluon jet tagging, 2 seeds) replicates the
HIGGS win directionally
($\sim$18-point accuracy gap, not yet significance-tested at this seed count).

\section{Introduction}

Variational quantum algorithms for HEP are bottlenecked by barren plateaus and by the cost of each circuit
evaluation, which makes fast convergence matter more than in classical optimization. Q-MAML~\citep{leeqmaml2025}
(arXiv:2501.05906) trains a classical ``Learner'' network to predict PQC initial parameters via a
meta-objective backpropagated with no inner-loop adaptation (Algorithm~1). The paper validates this on two
VQE settings only: Heisenberg XYZ and molecular Hamiltonians.

Two gaps follow. Q-MAML has never been tested on HEP classification, the explicit motivation of the 2025
GSoC project this work builds on: auditing that codebase (\S\ref{sec:audit}), we find neither of its two
attempts actually runs the published algorithm. And it has never been tested on a Hamiltonian that is itself
particle physics: lattice field theories, which is what the 2026 proposal (QMLHEP8) motivating this work
asks for. \S\ref{sec:audit}--\ref{sec:bp-combined} close the first gap, \S\ref{sec:genuine-hep} the second.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item Audit of the GSoC 2025 codebase: a detached-gradient bug and an algorithmic divergence from
    Algorithm~1 (\S\ref{sec:audit}).
    \item A paper-faithful Algorithm~1/2 implementation, validated against the paper's own two VQE settings
    before extension to classification (\S\ref{sec:method}, \S\ref{sec:vqe-molecule}--\ref{sec:vqe-heisenberg}).
    \item Q-MAML vs.\ classical inits (zero, $\pi$, reduced-domain uniform, Gaussian) vs.\ the original GSoC
    baseline, across qubit counts, with gradient-norm diagnostics (\S\ref{sec:classification}).
    \item A McClean-et-al.\ barren-plateau variance-scaling scan in both the VQE and classification domains
    (\S\ref{sec:bp-vqe}--\ref{sec:bp-combined}).
    \item A pilot on ten lattice/field-theory (and adjacent) Hamiltonians (Schwinger, $\mathbb{Z}_2$ LGT,
    $\phi^4$ scalar field, SU(2) LGT, collective neutrino oscillations, SUSY QM, $\mathbb{D}_8$ LGT, light
    nuclei in lattice pionless EFT, scalar Yukawa coupling, LMG), what
    the GSoC 2026 proposal asks for and untested by the original paper or contributions 1--4, with several
    findings that revise claims made earlier in this paper (\S\ref{sec:genuine-hep}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Auditing the Prior Codebase}
\label{sec:audit}

Two independent GSoC 2025 attempts to apply Q-MAML to HEP classification both deviate from the published
algorithm, in different ways:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Quark-gluon pipeline}: runs several steps of gradient descent on the PQC parameters before
    computing the outer (meta) loss, then stops gradient flow through the adapted parameters. Writing
    $\theta_0$ for the Learner's prediction and $\theta_T$ for the parameters after $T$ inner steps, the
    forward pass uses $\theta_T$ but the backward pass sees
    $\partial\theta_T/\partial\theta_0 \equiv \mathbb{1}$ (stop-gradient), i.e.\ a first-order-MAML-style
    variant, not the paper's inner-loop-free Algorithm~1.
    \item \textbf{Higgs pipeline}: the per-task loss is converted to a plain (non-differentiable) scalar
    before being accumulated and only re-wrapped as a fresh, disconnected tensor at the end for
    backpropagation, so the gradient the Learner receives carries no information about the training data.
    The Learner's weights never update across the reported 10 training epochs. A companion notebook is
    gradient-correct but only ever trains on one constant task embedding, which is not meta-learning in any
    meaningful sense.
    \item \textbf{Engineering}: missing package-init files broke the top-level entry point's own imports as
    committed; a pinned third-party model snapshot was incompatible with current library releases; a
    configuration module created a directory named after its own placeholder path string on import.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Paper-faithful Q-MAML (Algorithm 1/2)}

Per task $T_i$ with cost $l_{T_i}(\theta)=\langle\psi(\theta)|\hat H|\psi(\theta)\rangle$, a Learner $h_W$
maps task descriptor $\phi_i\to\theta=h_W(\phi_i)$, trained via (paper's Eq.~2)
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:metaobj}
\arg\min_W \sum_{T_i\sim p(T)} l_{T_i}\big(g(h_W(\phi_i))\big),
\end{equation}
backpropagated with no inner-loop adaptation (Eq.~3: the difference from true MAML). We keep
Eq.~\ref{eq:metaobj} exactly and swap the domain-specific pieces (Table~\ref{tab:method-map}).

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering\small
\caption{VQE setting (paper) $\to$ classification setting (this work).}
\label{tab:method-map}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.46\textwidth}p{0.46\textwidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Paper (VQE)} & \textbf{This work (classification)} \\
\midrule
Task = Hamiltonian instance, e.g.\ $J^i=[J_x,J_y,J_z]$ &
Task = quantile bin of a physics variable (reconstructed Higgs-candidate mass for HIGGS; jet transverse
momentum for quark-gluon), class-balanced support/query split \\
Descriptor $\phi_i=J^i$ & mean encoder embedding of the support set \\
$l_{T_i}(\theta)=\langle\psi|\hat H|\psi\rangle$ & $l_{T_i}(\theta)=\mathrm{CE}(\mathrm{PQC}_\theta(\text{support}_i), y_i)$ \\
PQC $g(\theta)$ & Same hardware-efficient ansatz family + classical encoder producing the encoding angles \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Pretraining (classification and VQE) shares this identical structure, differing only in the cost function.
Evaluation (Algorithm~2): freeze the Learner, set $\theta_0=h_{W^*}(\phi^*)$ for a held-out task, run
gradient descent on $l_{T^*}(\theta)$. This contrasts with the GSoC baseline (\S\ref{sec:audit}); both are
reported, neither treated as a straw man.

\subsection{Architecture}
Classical encoder (a convolutional backbone for jet images; a small MLP for tabular HIGGS features)
$\to$ PQC angles $\to$ hardware-efficient ansatz (single-qubit rotations plus a ring of entangling gates),
evaluated via state-vector simulation with adjoint differentiation where supported (parameter-shift
otherwise) $\to$ linear classification head. Learner: a 2-layer MLP mapping the task embedding to the PQC
weight tensor.

\subsection{Baselines}
Zero, $\pi$, reduced-domain uniform~\citep{wang2023trainability}, Gaussian~\citep{zhang2022escaping} init
(per-task adaptation with no Learner); the GSoC baseline (\S\ref{sec:audit}).

\subsection{Metrics, formally}
\label{sec:metrics}
For a held-out task with exact ground energy $E_0$ (sparse diagonalization) and PQC energy
$E_{\mathrm{PQC}}(\theta_t)$ at adaptation step $t$, we report a log-gap and a final relative error:
\begin{equation}
\Delta(t) = \log\!\Big(\big|E_{\mathrm{PQC}}(\theta_t) - E_0\big| + \epsilon\Big), \quad \epsilon=10^{-8},
\qquad
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}} = \frac{\big|E_{\mathrm{PQC}}(\theta_T) - E_0\big|}{\big\langle |E_0| \big\rangle_{\text{test tasks}}}.
\label{eq:metrics}
\end{equation}
Convergence speedup of Q-MAML over a classical scheme $A$, at a fixed error threshold $\Delta^\star$:
\begin{equation}
S = \frac{t_A(\Delta<\Delta^\star)}{t_{\text{Q-MAML}}(\Delta<\Delta^\star)}.
\label{eq:speedup}
\end{equation}
Classification significance: Welch's $t$-test and Cohen's $d=(\bar x_1-\bar x_2)/s_p$ with pooled standard
deviation $s_p=\sqrt{(s_1^2+s_2^2)/2}$, $n=4$ seeds per arm. Barren-plateau scan:
$\mathrm{Var}_{\theta,\text{task}}\!\big[\partial C/\partial\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}\big]$ at a fixed reference
parameter, sampled across many random inits/tasks, vs.\ qubit count at fixed depth.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Data}: HIGGS (UCI, 180k-row streamed subsample, primary); Quark-Gluon (ML4Sci, access
    requested, pending).
    \item \textbf{Tasks}: quantile-bin the Higgs-candidate mass into 6 bins; support=8/query=8 per bin; 3
    tasks/bin train (18 total), 2/bin test (12 total).
    \item \textbf{Qubits}: 4/6/8 (classification); 4--14 (VQE-domain barren-plateau scan).
    \item \textbf{Depth}: 2 (classification), 3/6 (VQE barren-plateau scan), 3 (Heisenberg), 7 (H2, matches
    paper).
    \item \textbf{Seeds}: 4 (classification headline).
    \item \textbf{Compute}: CPU only, state-vector simulation with adjoint differentiation (parameter-shift
    fallback).
    \item \textbf{Hyperparameters}: 5 inner-loop steps at inner learning rate 0.02, outer learning rate
    $5\times10^{-3}$, initial-weight scale 0.01 (classification); VQE pretraining learning rate 0.001 over 30
    epochs (matches paper); VQE adaptation learning rate 0.001 over 2000 iterations (matches paper).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Correction.} An earlier 2-seed pass showed the gap reversing (going negative) by 8 qubits. At $n=4$

Two other findings. Q-MAML's validation loss is worse (0.80--0.87, worsening with qubit count) than every

norm (1.36--1.61) is higher than classical schemes (0.27--0.64), the opposite of the paper's ``moderate norm
is better'' framing: here higher norm tracks with best accuracy at every qubit count.

features. Q-MAML again beats every classical scheme by a wide margin: validation accuracy $0.776\pm0.005$
vs.\ $0.594$--$0.599$ (zero/uniform/Gaussian, tightly clustered); meta-loss $0.308\pm0.003$ vs.\
$0.688$--$0.701$ (near chance level, $\ln2\approx0.693$, for every classical scheme). No `zero`-specific
anomaly here, matching HIGGS and unlike the VQE-domain fixed-point pattern. Only 2 seeds (effect size
$\sim6.8$ in raw terms, not a powered test): read as directional confirmation the HIGGS result generalizes,
not a sharper result than HIGGS's own 4-seed numbers.

\subsection{Combined takeaway}
\label{sec:bp-combined}
(1) Q-MAML's benefit is landscape placement, not a change in the barren-plateau scaling exponent, in either
domain. (2) HEP classification plateaus faster than VQE for every scheme tested: a challenge for the QMLHEP
direction generally, not specific to Q-MAML.

\subsection{Track E: collective neutrino oscillations (reformulated)}
\label{sec:track-e}
The literature this track is nominally based on~\citep{neutrinoqc2021} studies real-time evolution of a
flavor eigenstate, not a ground-state problem, and does not fit the Q-MAML formalism without a genuine
change of question. We ask instead for the ground state of the same class of collective (all-to-all)
neutrino Hamiltonian, the object studied in the adiabatic/spectral-split collective-oscillation
literature~\citep{duanfullerqian2010}, a different but still physically motivated question:
\begin{equation}
H(\theta,\mu) = \frac{\omega}{2}\sum_i\big(\cos2\theta\, Z_i + \sin2\theta\, X_i\big)
+ \frac{\mu}{N}\sum_{i<j}\big(X_iX_j+Y_iY_j+Z_iZ_j\big), \qquad \omega=1,
\label{eq:neutrino}
\end{equation}
$N{=}4$ neutrinos (4 qubits), task params mixing angle $\theta$ and interaction strength $\mu$. Verified
against two exact limits: at $\mu{=}0$, ground energy $=-N\omega/2$ for any $\theta$ (confirmed to
$\sim10^{-16}$); at $\theta{=}\mu{=}0$, ground state is exactly $|11\dots1\rangle$ (overlap $1.000000$).

\subsection{Track H: light nuclei in lattice pionless EFT}
\label{sec:track-h}

advantage is entirely in convergence \emph{shape}: Q-MAML descends smoothly from iteration 0

oscillatory trajectory} (common start $\Delta{\approx}0.75$, swinging to $-2.5$ and back to $-1.2$,

source paper's own non-learned approach: a \texttt{warm\_start\_nearby} baseline (adapting from a converged
nearby-task solution, approximating their "warm-started from a nearby statevector-simulator solution")
starts at $\Delta(0){=}{-2.10}$ and stays slightly behind Q-MAML throughout before reaching the same final
value.
\end{keyresult}

Q-MAML does \emph{not} start best here (Zero $\Delta(0){\approx}{-3.1}$, Uniform ${\approx}{-1.7}$ vs.\
Q-MAML's ${\approx}{-6.9}$, i.e.\ Q-MAML starts \emph{worse}), converging onto Zero's trajectory within
$\sim$10--15 iterations. Mechanistic explanation, not just observation: the task range is deliberately
narrowed to keep the displacement $\eta/(2m)$ small (Section~\ref{sec:track-i}'s verification), so every
ground state tested is near-vacuum, and Zero-init's circuit starts at exactly $|0000\rangle$, already
close for small displacements. $\pi$-init is a clear loser again, the second Hermitian-matrix Hamiltonian
(after the scalar field) where it fails badly despite strength on the Pauli-sum gauge-theory tracks.
\end{keyresult}

\subsection{Track J: Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick model}
\label{sec:track-j}
Flagged as nuclear-structure-adjacent rather than core HEP~\citep{hlvqe2023}, included for completeness.
Precisely differentiated from that source's "HL-VQE": despite the similar name, HL-VQE iteratively
co-optimizes an \emph{effective Hamiltonian} via orbital rotation, not a neural-network-learned
initialization; we run plain Q-MAML on their own un-rotated Hamiltonian, $J{=}3/2$ (2 qubits, 4 states,
matching their own smallest showcase scale). Verified via the exact su(2) algebra on the constructed
generators and the analytically-known $V{=}0$ limit.

\begin{keyresult}
\textbf{Genuine null result, not a close call.} All five schemes reach the float64 precision floor
(log-gap $-16.8$ to $-17.5$), and the ranking among them is within run-to-run noise: the convergence
trajectories run together in one noisy bundle for the entire 150-iteration budget, never separating,
unlike every other track in this paper. Likely explanation: 4 states on 2 qubits may be too small a
Hilbert space for a bad initialization to get meaningfully stuck. The clear next step is a \emph{larger}
$J$ (more qubits), not more seeds: this benchmark needs to get harder before an initialization-scheme
comparison here is informative.
\end{keyresult}

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{Why does the inner-loop-free algorithm beat the GSoC baseline (inner loop + stop-gradient)?}
Original hypothesis: the stop-gradient trick gives a weaker training signal.

Tested directly with an ablation sweeping the inner-loop step count from 0 to 20 (Table~\ref{tab:ablation},
GSoC baseline otherwise unchanged): final meta-loss is flat across the sweep and validation accuracy
\emph{increases} with more inner steps, opposite of the hypothesis. Gradient norm scales up with more inner
steps and tracks better, not worse, accuracy. Hypothesis retracted. Also, the GSoC baseline at zero inner
steps does not match Q-MAML's own numbers (meta-loss $0.68$ vs.\ $0.364$) even accounting for the known
support/query evaluation-split difference between the two implementations: why Q-MAML beats the GSoC
baseline remains open.

\textbf{Does the classification advantage shrink with qubit count, as \S\ref{sec:bp-classification}
predicts?} Partially. A 2-seed pass showed a reversal by 8 qubits; at 4 seeds it does not hold
(\S\ref{sec:classification}, Table~\ref{tab:correction-gap}): the gap trends smaller, directionally
consistent, but not confirmed. Solid at every qubit count: the win vs.\ Gaussian and the GSoC baseline.

\textbf{``Moderate gradient norm.''} The paper frames moderate norm as good conditioning. Q-MAML has the
\emph{highest} norm and the best accuracy: the opposite of a monotonic ``moderate is best'' rule. Does not
contradict the underlying point (avoiding vanishing gradients), but ``moderate'' should not be read as
``closer to some middle value is always better.''

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{\S\ref{sec:genuine-hep} is single-seed} except where noted (Track B's $V{=}10$ point,
    Track C's interpolation-split headline: both 3-seed). Multi-seed replication of the rest is the top
    priority for this section.
    \item \textbf{Track B}: the $V$-sweep refuted the penalty-dominance hypothesis rather than confirming
    it. Untried: annealing $V$ during pretraining; separating penalty/physical energy into adaptively-
    weighted loss terms; the structurally gauge-invariant ansatz comparison (the track's originally intended
    angle).
    \item \textbf{Track A}: depth re-tune at $L{=}3$ is two spot-checks (depth 6, 9), not a scan; the
    $L{=}4$ failure (pretraining non-convergence) is one spot-check, not resolved. A joint
    depth$\times$pretrain-budget$\times$qubit-count scan is needed.
    \item \textbf{Track C}: phase-stratified test is single-seed; broken$\to$symmetric error is large enough
    ($85$--$97\%$) that seed noise could reorder the ranking, though ``nobody generalizes well here'' is
    likely to hold.
    \item \textbf{Track D}: does not reproduce the source paper's qubit-reduction trick, its hadron-mass-
    ratio calculation, or $N{=}4$ (baryon scale); $N{=}2$ only, single seed. Its unusually clean result is
    itself a reason to check whether it holds at $N{=}4$ rather than assume it does.
    \item \textbf{Track E}: the entire premise is a reformulation (ground state instead of the cited
    literature's real-time dynamics), see \S\ref{sec:track-e}. Single seed; none of the five schemes had
    converged within the iteration budget, so the final-value gap may still narrow with more iterations, as
    it did in \S\ref{sec:vqe-heisenberg}.
    \item \textbf{Track F}: single seed; 9 held-out tasks split unevenly across three superpotentials by
    random draw (2--4 each), not a balanced/stratified split; uses a fixed-depth hardware-efficient ansatz,
    not the source paper's adaptive-VQE operator-selection approach.
    \item \textbf{Track G}: does not reproduce the source paper's qubit-reduced (matter-removal) Hamiltonian
    or its trapped-ion qudit gate set, since several of that reduction's intermediate equations could not
    be extracted with full confidence; uses the original, un-reduced Hamiltonian at $N{=}2$ instead. Single
    seed; no plaquette term exists in a 1D chain at any $N$.
    \item \textbf{Track H}: $N{=}2$ for all three nuclei, single seed, task parameters $(t,C,D)$ swept
    continuously rather than fixed to the source paper's own experimentally-calibrated values. The
    \texttt{warm\_start\_nearby} baseline approximates, not reproduces, the source paper's own warm-start
    protocol.
    \item \textbf{Track I}: single seed; task range deliberately narrowed to the boson truncation's
    accurate regime, meaning this specifically tests the near-vacuum corner of the model's parameter space,
    plausibly why Zero-init is competitive here unlike elsewhere in this paper.
    \item \textbf{Track J}: single seed, $J{=}3/2$ only; no comparison against HL-VQE's own reported
    numbers was attempted, and given the null result at this scale, a larger $J$ is needed before such a
    comparison would be meaningful either way.
    \item \textbf{Ansatz}: nine of the ten tracks use the same hardware-efficient ansatz, not the
    symmetry-preserving circuits available for several of them (A/B/D/G); Track H uses UCCSD instead,
    the paper's only ansatz-family comparison. Isolates init-scheme comparison from ansatz-design
    confounds on nine tracks, but results there are about relative init quality on a fixed ansatz, not
    best achievable VQE performance.
    \item \textbf{CPU-only simulation} caps qubit count/depth (14 for the cheap VQE barren-plateau scan, 8
    for classification) below where plateaus are most severe on real hardware or GPU-scale simulation.
    \item \textbf{4 seeds} (HIGGS classification) resolves significance vs.\ Gaussian/GSoC baseline and
    catches the 2-seed false positive, but not the vs.-best-classical comparison ($p=0.08$--$0.29$). 8--10
    seeds is the next step. \textbf{Quark-gluon classification is 2 seeds only} (\S\ref{sec:classification}):
    the $\sim$18-point accuracy gap is directionally consistent with HIGGS but not independently
    significance-tested.
    \item \textbf{Reconstructed Heisenberg ansatz} (paper's appendix circuit unavailable): the molecule study
    (paper's exact ansatz) is the stronger evidence.
    \item \textbf{Task-generation}: single-variable quantile binning untested for sensitivity to bin count or
    binning variable.
    \item \textbf{Barren-plateau diagnostic scope}: single reference-parameter gradient per sample (standard
    McClean protocol), not a full Fisher-information/effective-dimension analysis.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

We tested whether Q-MAML~\citep{leeqmaml2025} improves PQC trainability in a setting it was never evaluated
on (HEP classification), after finding neither prior GSoC attempt actually ran the published algorithm. Our
system matches the paper's own VQE results (reconstructed and exact-ansatz settings) before extending to
classification, where it beats classical init and the prior codebase with statistical significance at every
qubit count tested. A barren-plateau scan bounds the claim: the benefit is landscape placement, not plateau
resistance, and classification plateaus faster than the VQE setting the paper was validated on.

Beyond classification, a pilot on ten lattice/field-theory (and adjacent) Hamiltonians
(\S\ref{sec:genuine-hep}), what the GSoC 2026 proposal asks for, gives a mostly-positive, diagnosed result:
clear wins on six (Schwinger, $\phi^4$ scalar field, SU(2) LGT, collective neutrino oscillations, SUSY QM,
$\mathbb{D}_8$ LGT), ties for best on two (light nuclei: same final energy for every scheme, Q-MAML faster;
Yukawa: Q-MAML ties Zero-init, both at the precision floor), a genuine null result on one (LMG, too small a
system to differentiate any scheme), and worst-scheme on one ($\mathbb{Z}_2$ LGT; Gauss-law penalty
implicated but not confirmed as sufficient cause). Track D and Track G (independent Abelian and non-Abelian
confirmations of Track A's gauge-eliminated-analytically pattern), Track F (the source paper's own
hand-picked physics init loses to generic random init), and Track H (the first ansatz-family comparison in
this paper, and the first track where convergence \emph{shape} rather than final value carries the whole
result) are the sharpest new results; Tracks E and I required reformulating their cited literature's
question from real-time dynamics to a ground-state search, stated explicitly rather than glossed over.
Findings that revise earlier sections: the zero-parameter fixed point is not universal and, per Track E, is
not simply a Pauli-sum-vs.-Hermitian-matrix property either; no classical init is a safe default across
Hamiltonian families the way Q-MAML nearly is, under interpolation, not extrapolation. Which structural
property of a Hamiltonian predicts whether learned initialization helps is now a concrete question with six
wins, two ties, one null result, one diagnosed loss, and several partly-tested mechanisms, rather than an
open guess.
\todo{more seeds (8-10) for both classification datasets, qubit counts beyond 8, Track D at $N{=}4$, Track J
at larger $J$, before submission.}
