\begin{abstract}
We re-test Q-MAML~\citep{leeqmaml2025} (AAAI 2025), a meta-learned PQC initializer, on HEP problems it was
never evaluated on. First, we audit the ML4Sci GSoC 2025 codebase that applies it to HEP classification and
find both attempts broken: in one, the training loss is detached from the computation graph before
backpropagation, so the learner network never actually updates; the other runs a first-order-MAML-style
inner loop, not the paper's published algorithm. We build a paper-faithful re-implementation and calibrate
it against the paper's own VQE settings at its published 2000-iteration budget (table below).
Applied to HIGGS classification, a novel setting for this algorithm, it gets the best validation accuracy at
every qubit count tested, significant vs.\ Gaussian init and the GSoC baseline ($p<0.05$, $n=4$ seeds). A
McClean-et-al.\ barren-plateau scan shows no scheme, including ours, changes the variance-scaling exponent;
classification cost functions plateau $\sim$8 orders of magnitude faster than the VQE costs the paper was
validated on. Finally we run ten genuine lattice/field-theory (and adjacent) Hamiltonian pilots (Schwinger
model, $\mathbb{Z}_2$ lattice gauge theory, $\phi^4$ scalar field, SU(2) lattice gauge theory, collective
neutrino oscillations, supersymmetric quantum mechanics, non-Abelian $\mathbb{D}_8$ lattice gauge theory,
light nuclei in lattice pionless EFT, scalar Yukawa coupling, and the Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick model), now
$n{=}3$-seed replicated for all ten: four robust wins, four statistical ties (two of which looked like
clean single-seed wins until replicated -- direct evidence for why we replicate), a seed-robust null result
(too small a system to differentiate any scheme, and still null at a larger system size), and one diagnosed
loss. \textbf{The diagnosed loss turns out to be an ansatz artifact, not a Q-MAML weakness}: switching to a
structurally gauge-invariant ansatz (vs.\ the headline penalty-enforced one) makes Q-MAML the best scheme at
all three seeds, $3\times$--$10\times$ ahead of the best classical scheme. A second qubit-count axis, tried
on two tracks, gives different outcomes: one track's machine-precision win at its smallest size gives way
at 8 qubits to a seed-unstable Q-MAML (best at one of three seeds, catastrophic at another) while a
classical scheme becomes the reliable choice, whereas another track's win is more decisive at 6 qubits than
at 4 -- Q-MAML's advantage is seed-, scale-, and ansatz-dependent, not fixed.
A quantum-Fisher-information probe gives the paper's ``landscape placement, not plateau resistance''
explanation quantitative content: Q-MAML's predicted initializations sit at consistently higher effective
dimension than classical schemes at every qubit count tested. A second classification dataset (quark-gluon
jet tagging, 2 seeds) replicates the HIGGS win directionally ($\sim$18-point accuracy gap, not yet
significance-tested at this seed count).

\section{Introduction}

Variational quantum algorithms for HEP are bottlenecked by barren plateaus and by the cost of each circuit
evaluation, which makes fast convergence matter more than in classical optimization. Q-MAML~\citep{leeqmaml2025}
(arXiv:2501.05906) trains a classical ``Learner'' network to predict PQC initial parameters via a
meta-objective backpropagated with no inner-loop adaptation (Algorithm~1). The paper validates this on two
VQE settings only: Heisenberg XYZ and molecular Hamiltonians.

Two gaps follow. Q-MAML has never been tested on HEP classification, the explicit motivation of the 2025
GSoC project this work builds on: auditing that codebase (\S\ref{sec:audit}), we find neither of its two
attempts actually runs the published algorithm. And it has never been tested on a Hamiltonian that is itself
particle physics: lattice field theories, which is what the 2026 proposal (QMLHEP8) motivating this work
asks for. \S\ref{sec:audit}--\ref{sec:bp-combined} close the first gap, \S\ref{sec:genuine-hep} the second.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item Audit of the GSoC 2025 codebase: a detached-gradient bug and an algorithmic divergence from
    Algorithm~1 (\S\ref{sec:audit}).
    \item A paper-faithful Algorithm~1/2 implementation, validated against the paper's own two VQE settings
    before extension to classification (\S\ref{sec:method}, \S\ref{sec:vqe-molecule}--\ref{sec:vqe-heisenberg}).
    \item Q-MAML vs.\ classical inits (zero, $\pi$, reduced-domain uniform, Gaussian) vs.\ the original GSoC
    baseline, across qubit counts, with gradient-norm diagnostics (\S\ref{sec:classification}).
    \item A McClean-et-al.\ barren-plateau variance-scaling scan in both the VQE and classification domains
    (\S\ref{sec:bp-vqe}--\ref{sec:bp-combined}).
    \item A pilot on ten lattice/field-theory (and adjacent) Hamiltonians (Schwinger, $\mathbb{Z}_2$ LGT,
    $\phi^4$ scalar field, SU(2) LGT, collective neutrino oscillations, SUSY QM, $\mathbb{D}_8$ LGT, light
    nuclei in lattice pionless EFT, scalar Yukawa coupling, LMG), what
    the GSoC 2026 proposal asks for and untested by the original paper or contributions 1--4, with several
    findings that revise claims made earlier in this paper (\S\ref{sec:genuine-hep}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Auditing the Prior Codebase}
\label{sec:audit}

Two independent GSoC 2025 attempts to apply Q-MAML to HEP classification both deviate from the published
algorithm, in different ways:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Quark-gluon pipeline}: runs several steps of gradient descent on the PQC parameters before
    computing the outer (meta) loss, then stops gradient flow through the adapted parameters. Writing
    $\theta_0$ for the Learner's prediction and $\theta_T$ for the parameters after $T$ inner steps, the
    forward pass uses $\theta_T$ but the backward pass sees
    $\partial\theta_T/\partial\theta_0 \equiv \mathbb{1}$ (stop-gradient), i.e.\ a first-order-MAML-style
    variant, not the paper's inner-loop-free Algorithm~1.
    \item \textbf{Higgs pipeline}: the per-task loss is converted to a plain (non-differentiable) scalar
    before being accumulated and only re-wrapped as a fresh, disconnected tensor at the end for
    backpropagation, so the gradient the Learner receives carries no information about the training data.
    The Learner's weights never update across the reported 10 training epochs. A companion notebook is
    gradient-correct but only ever trains on one constant task embedding, which is not meta-learning in any
    meaningful sense.
    \item \textbf{Engineering}: missing package-init files broke the top-level entry point's own imports as
    committed; a pinned third-party model snapshot was incompatible with current library releases; a
    configuration module created a directory named after its own placeholder path string on import.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Paper-faithful Q-MAML (Algorithm 1/2)}

Per task $T_i$ with cost $l_{T_i}(\theta)=\langle\psi(\theta)|\hat H|\psi(\theta)\rangle$, a Learner $h_W$
maps task descriptor $\phi_i\to\theta=h_W(\phi_i)$, trained via (paper's Eq.~2)
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:metaobj}
\arg\min_W \sum_{T_i\sim p(T)} l_{T_i}\big(g(h_W(\phi_i))\big),
\end{equation}
backpropagated with no inner-loop adaptation (Eq.~3: the difference from true MAML). We keep
Eq.~\ref{eq:metaobj} exactly and swap the domain-specific pieces (Table~\ref{tab:method-map}).

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering\small
\caption{VQE setting (paper) $\to$ classification setting (this work).}
\label{tab:method-map}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.46\textwidth}p{0.46\textwidth}}
\toprule
\textbf{Paper (VQE)} & \textbf{This work (classification)} \\
\midrule
Task = Hamiltonian instance, e.g.\ $J^i=[J_x,J_y,J_z]$ &
Task = quantile bin of a physics variable (reconstructed Higgs-candidate mass for HIGGS; jet transverse
momentum for quark-gluon), class-balanced support/query split \\
Descriptor $\phi_i=J^i$ & mean encoder embedding of the support set \\
$l_{T_i}(\theta)=\langle\psi|\hat H|\psi\rangle$ & $l_{T_i}(\theta)=\mathrm{CE}(\mathrm{PQC}_\theta(\text{support}_i), y_i)$ \\
PQC $g(\theta)$ & Same hardware-efficient ansatz family + classical encoder producing the encoding angles \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Pretraining (classification and VQE) shares this identical structure, differing only in the cost function.
Evaluation (Algorithm~2): freeze the Learner, set $\theta_0=h_{W^*}(\phi^*)$ for a held-out task, run
gradient descent on $l_{T^*}(\theta)$. This contrasts with the GSoC baseline (\S\ref{sec:audit}); both are
reported, neither treated as a straw man.

\subsection{Architecture}
Classical encoder (a convolutional backbone for jet images; a small MLP for tabular HIGGS features)
$\to$ PQC angles $\to$ hardware-efficient ansatz (single-qubit rotations plus a ring of entangling gates),
evaluated via state-vector simulation with adjoint differentiation where supported (parameter-shift
otherwise) $\to$ linear classification head. Learner: a 2-layer MLP mapping the task embedding to the PQC
weight tensor.

\subsection{Baselines}
Zero, $\pi$, reduced-domain uniform~\citep{wang2023trainability}, Gaussian~\citep{zhang2022escaping} init
(per-task adaptation with no Learner); the GSoC baseline (\S\ref{sec:audit}).

\subsection{Metrics, formally}
\label{sec:metrics}
For a held-out task with exact ground energy $E_0$ (sparse diagonalization) and PQC energy
$E_{\mathrm{PQC}}(\theta_t)$ at adaptation step $t$, we report a log-gap and a final relative error:
\begin{equation}
\Delta(t) = \log\!\Big(\big|E_{\mathrm{PQC}}(\theta_t) - E_0\big| + \epsilon\Big), \quad \epsilon=10^{-8},
\qquad
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}} = \frac{\big|E_{\mathrm{PQC}}(\theta_T) - E_0\big|}{\big\langle |E_0| \big\rangle_{\text{test tasks}}}.
\label{eq:metrics}
\end{equation}
Convergence speedup of Q-MAML over a classical scheme $A$, at a fixed error threshold $\Delta^\star$:
\begin{equation}
S = \frac{t_A(\Delta<\Delta^\star)}{t_{\text{Q-MAML}}(\Delta<\Delta^\star)}.
\label{eq:speedup}
\end{equation}
Classification significance: Welch's $t$-test and Cohen's $d=(\bar x_1-\bar x_2)/s_p$ with pooled standard
deviation $s_p=\sqrt{(s_1^2+s_2^2)/2}$, $n=4$ seeds per arm. Barren-plateau scan:
$\mathrm{Var}_{\theta,\text{task}}\!\big[\partial C/\partial\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}\big]$ at a fixed reference
parameter, sampled across many random inits/tasks, vs.\ qubit count at fixed depth.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Data}: HIGGS (UCI, 180k-row streamed subsample, primary); Quark-Gluon (ML4Sci, access
    requested, pending).
    \item \textbf{Tasks}: quantile-bin the Higgs-candidate mass into 6 bins; support=8/query=8 per bin; 3
    tasks/bin train (18 total), 2/bin test (12 total).
    \item \textbf{Qubits}: 4/6/8 (classification); 4--14 (VQE-domain barren-plateau scan).
    \item \textbf{Depth}: 2 (classification), 3/6 (VQE barren-plateau scan), 3 (Heisenberg), 7 (H2, matches
    paper).
    \item \textbf{Seeds}: 4 (classification headline).
    \item \textbf{Compute}: CPU only, state-vector simulation with adjoint differentiation (parameter-shift
    fallback).
    \item \textbf{Hyperparameters}: 5 inner-loop steps at inner learning rate 0.02, outer learning rate
    $5\times10^{-3}$, initial-weight scale 0.01 (classification); VQE pretraining learning rate 0.001 over 30
    epochs (matches paper); VQE adaptation learning rate 0.001 over 2000 iterations (matches paper).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Barren-plateau scaling, VQE domain}
\label{sec:bp-vqe}
Qubits 4--14, depths $\{3,6\}$, 80 samples per point. Zero/$\pi$ (constant) init show zero variance
trivially (no sample-to-sample variation, not plateau resistance). Among the random schemes (Q-MAML,
uniform, Gaussian): no exponential decay over 4--10 qubits at depth 3, likely too shallow/small for the
asymptotic regime. Q-MAML's variance is comparable to or larger than classical schemes at every qubit count:
no evidence it changes the scaling exponent; its VQE advantage (\S\ref{sec:vqe-heisenberg}) is landscape
placement, not plateau resistance.

\begin{figure}[ht]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../final_model/results/barren_plateau/variance_scaling.png}
\caption{Barren-plateau variance scaling by init scheme, VQE domain (depths 3 and 6).}
\label{fig:bp-vqe}
\end{figure}

\begin{keyresult}
\textbf{A second, independent diagnostic gives ``landscape placement'' a quantitative meaning.} The scan
above uses a single reference-parameter gradient per sample (the standard McClean et al.\ protocol),
which cannot distinguish ``degenerate everywhere'' from ``degenerate along one direction, rich along
others.'' We additionally compute the quantum Fisher information matrix (via PennyLane's
\texttt{metric\_tensor}, block-diagonal approximation) at each sampled parameter point and summarize it as
a participation ratio $PR=(\sum\lambda)^2/\sum\lambda^2$ normalized by parameter count -- an effective
dimension: how many directions in parameter space the circuit can actually distinguish, independent of and
complementary to a single-coordinate gradient. Qubits $\{4,6,8\}$, depth 3, Heisenberg XYZ ansatz, 8 samples
per (qubit count, scheme).

\begin{center}
\captionof{table}{Effective dimension (participation ratio / \# parameters), Heisenberg XYZ ansatz.}
\label{tab:fisher-effdim}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Qubits & Q-MAML & Uniform & Gaussian \\
\midrule
4 & $0.933\pm0.011$ & $0.683\pm0.011$ & $0.715\pm0.033$ \\
6 & $0.934\pm0.025$ & $0.687\pm0.011$ & $0.726\pm0.019$ \\
8 & $\mathbf{0.958}\pm0.016$ & $0.693\pm0.011$ & $0.733\pm0.018$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Q-MAML's predicted initializations sit at consistently higher effective dimension ($93$--$96\%$ of the
maximum possible rank) than Uniform ($68$--$69\%$) or Gaussian ($72$--$73\%$) at every qubit count tested,
and the gap does not shrink with qubit count -- if anything Q-MAML's edge widens slightly at 8 qubits. This
is consistent with, and gives quantitative content to, the ``landscape placement'' explanation above: by
this independent metric, Q-MAML's initialization is not merely comparable in gradient magnitude but sits in
a genuinely less-degenerate region of parameter space than random classical schemes, a distinction the
single-reference-gradient scan alone cannot make. Reduced qubit range (4--8 vs.\ the gradient scan's 4--14)
and sample count (8 vs.\ 80 per point) for tractability -- \texttt{metric\_tensor} is markedly more
expensive per sample than a single gradient component; extending this to the full range and to the
classification domain is future work.
\end{keyresult}

\subsection{Barren-plateau scaling, classification domain}
\label{sec:bp-classification}
\label{sec:bp-higgs}
HIGGS.

\begin{keyresult}
All three schemes (Q-MAML, uniform, Gaussian) show $\sim$8 orders of magnitude exponential decay from 4 to
10 qubits: a textbook plateau, unlike the flat VQE trend at the same qubit range, consistent with
classification cross-entropy behaving as a ``global'' cost function~\citep{cerezo2021costfunction}. By 10
qubits every scheme collapses to the same floor, $\sim10^{-35}$.
\end{keyresult}

\emph{(i) $N{=}3$ at depth 6 / 300 iterations} (vs.\ depth 4 / 250), all three seeds finished: every scheme
now converges, confirming the depth-4 ``everyone fails'' result was a budget artifact. Relative error (\%),
mean $\pm$ std over seeds 0/1/2: $\pi$ $\mathbf{0.28}\pm0.07$ ($0.18/0.31/0.33$), Q-MAML $2.02\pm0.39$

seed; Q-MAML is second at seeds 1 and 2 and third at seed 0 (behind $\pi$ and Uniform), $5.7\times$--$8.9\times$
behind $\pi$ per seed ($7.6\times$ on average), and is the most consistent of the non-$\pi$ schemes (Uniform
and Gaussian each swing from $\sim$1--4\% to $\sim$16--22\%). So at a converging budget there is no Q-MAML
margin at $N{=}3$ either: it is a stable second-tier scheme behind $\pi$.
\emph{(ii) $N{=}4$ with pretrain learning rate $0.002$ and 100 epochs} (vs.\ $0.01$ and 40), all three
seeds finished: Q-MAML $312\%$ / $2.9\%$ / $450\%$ at seeds 0/1/2, against $23.3\%$ / $2.91\%$ / $450.4\%$ at
the original setting; $\pi$ is unchanged at $3.5$--$6.0\%$. The lower learning rate that repaired Track A's
$L{=}4$ pretraining therefore does not fix Track D's bad seeds: seed 0 got \emph{worse} ($23\%\to312\%$), and
seeds 1 and 2 agree with the original setting to four digits ($2.90$ vs.\ $2.91\%$; $450.36$ vs.\ $450.36\%$)
despite 2.5$\times$ more epochs at a fifth of the learning rate. We do not know why. One reading is that
adaptation from Q-MAML's prediction reaches the same final basin however it was pretrained, but this was not
tested, and pretraining diagnostics (final energy, gradient norm) were not recorded, so these runs cannot
show whether pretraining converged. What the pair of follow-ups does establish is that neither a converging
$N{=}3$ budget nor the Track A learning-rate fix restores a Q-MAML margin, and Track D at $N{=}3$/$4$ is best
summarized as: $\pi$ is the reliable scheme, Q-MAML is competitive-to-erratic, and the $N{=}2$ advantage does
not extend.
\end{keyresult}

\subsection{Track E: collective neutrino oscillations (reformulated)}
\label{sec:track-e}
The literature this track is nominally based on~\citep{neutrinoqc2021} studies real-time evolution of a
flavor eigenstate, not a ground-state problem, and does not fit the Q-MAML formalism without a genuine
change of question. We ask instead for the ground state of the same class of collective (all-to-all)
neutrino Hamiltonian, the object studied in the adiabatic/spectral-split collective-oscillation
literature~\citep{duanfullerqian2010}, a different but still physically motivated question:
\begin{equation}
H(\theta,\mu) = \frac{\omega}{2}\sum_i\big(\cos2\theta\, Z_i + \sin2\theta\, X_i\big)
+ \frac{\mu}{N}\sum_{i<j}\big(X_iX_j+Y_iY_j+Z_iZ_j\big), \qquad \omega=1,
\label{eq:neutrino}
\end{equation}
$N{=}4$ neutrinos (4 qubits), task params mixing angle $\theta$ and interaction strength $\mu$. Verified
against two exact limits: at $\mu{=}0$, ground energy $=-N\omega/2$ for any $\theta$ (confirmed to
$\sim10^{-16}$); at $\theta{=}\mu{=}0$, ground state is exactly $|11\dots1\rangle$ (overlap $1.000000$).

\subsection{Track H: light nuclei in lattice pionless EFT}
\label{sec:track-h}
Deuteron, triton, and helium-3 via lattice pionless EFT~\citep{nucleareft2026}, the best-precedented
VQE-for-physics track here (lineage of the 2018 IBM deuteron result). $N{=}2$ sites for all three nuclei
(8 qubits: 4 proton $+$ 4 neutron spin-orbitals). \textbf{First new ansatz family in this paper}: a
particle-number-conserving UCCSD circuit (PennyLane's built-in template), not a hardware-efficient one:
proton and neutron excitations generated independently so no excitation crosses species, exact conservation
by construction rather than by penalty (contrast Track B's Gauss-law penalty). Verified: Hermiticity;
proton number $N_p$ and neutron number $N_n$ each exactly commute with $H$. An unplanned check: helium-3
came out less bound than triton at matched couplings, matching the real experimental fact (driven by
Coulomb repulsion between helium-3's two protons), which fell out of the construction, not designed in.

\begin{keyresult}
\textbf{Every scheme converges to the identical final energy} (0.89\% relative error, to 3 decimal places,
for all five classical/learned schemes and a sixth baseline below): a first in this paper. UCCSD's
particle-conserving structure (2--4 variational amplitudes vs.\ 48 angles for the hardware-efficient
ansatz) appears simple enough that initialization stops mattering for the \emph{final} answer. The
advantage is entirely in convergence \emph{shape}: Q-MAML descends smoothly from iteration 0
($\Delta(0){=}{-2.37}$), while every classical scheme collapses onto \emph{one shared, strongly
oscillatory trajectory} (common start $\Delta{\approx}0.75$, swinging to $-2.5$ and back to $-1.2$,
ringing for $\sim$80 iterations) before damping into the same final value. Q-MAML also modestly beats the
source paper's own non-learned approach: a \texttt{warm\_start\_nearby} baseline (adapting from a converged
nearby-task solution, approximating their "warm-started from a nearby statevector-simulator solution")
starts at $\Delta(0){=}{-2.10}$ and stays slightly behind Q-MAML throughout before reaching the same final
value.
\end{keyresult}

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{../final_model/results/nuclear/adaptation_convergence.png}

\subsection{Track J: Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick model}
\label{sec:track-j}
Flagged as nuclear-structure-adjacent rather than core HEP~\citep{hlvqe2023}, included for completeness.
Precisely differentiated from that source's "HL-VQE": despite the similar name, HL-VQE iteratively
co-optimizes an \emph{effective Hamiltonian} via orbital rotation, not a neural-network-learned
initialization; we run plain Q-MAML on their own un-rotated Hamiltonian, $J{=}3/2$ (2 qubits, 4 states,
matching their own smallest showcase scale). Verified via the exact su(2) algebra on the constructed
generators and the analytically-known $V{=}0$ limit.

\begin{keyresult}
\textbf{Genuine null result, not a close call.} All five schemes reach the float64 precision floor
(log-gap $-16.8$ to $-17.5$), and the ranking among them is within run-to-run noise: the convergence
trajectories run together in one noisy bundle for the entire 150-iteration budget, never separating,
unlike every other track in this paper. Likely explanation: 4 states on 2 qubits may be too small a
Hilbert space for a bad initialization to get meaningfully stuck. The clear next step is a \emph{larger}
$J$ (more qubits), not more seeds: this benchmark needs to get harder before an initialization-scheme
comparison here is informative.
\end{keyresult}

\begin{keyresult}
\textbf{Multi-seed replication ($n{=}3$) confirms the null result.} Every scheme, at every seed, lands in
the $6\times10^{-9}$--$6\times10^{-8}$ range -- tighter than the schemes' own seed-to-seed spread, i.e.\
initialization scheme explains less variance than random seed does at this scale. This reinforces rather
than revises the conclusion above: this benchmark is uninformative about initialization quality until $J$
is increased (still not attempted -- see \S\ref{sec:limitations}), not because the null result itself is in
doubt.
\end{keyresult}

\begin{keyresult}
\textbf{The suggested next step (larger $J$) does not fix the benchmark.} $J{=}3.5$ ($2J{+}1{=}8$ states,
3 qubits, no Hilbert-space padding needed -- the same reasoning the original $J{=}1.5$ choice used),
Hermiticity/su(2)-algebra/$V{=}0$-limit re-verified before running, $n{=}3$ seeds. Every scheme still lands
at the float64 precision floor ($\sim10^{-6}$--$10^{-8}$ relative error) at every seed; Q-MAML shows no
separation from Zero, $\pi$, or Gaussian. \textbf{This revises the diagnosis, not just the qubit count}: the
original hypothesis was that $J{=}1.5$'s 4-state Hilbert space was too small for a bad initialization to get
meaningfully stuck; $J{=}3.5$'s 8-state space is twice as large and still shows no scheme separation, so
Hilbert-space size alone is not the operative variable. Whether an even larger $J$ would eventually
differentiate schemes, or whether this task family is simply easy for VQE regardless of size, remains open.
\end{keyresult}

seed, reduced protocol): the hyperparameters that worked at one system size did not transfer to the next,
at either of two consecutive sizes. \\
\midrule
Track B's failure is real; one leading mechanism (penalty dominance) is refuted by the $V$-sweep, not
confirmed. What holds: penalty-\emph{enforced} gauge invariance is worse for Q-MAML than either eliminating
gauge structure analytically (A) or having none (C), regardless of penalty strength. \textbf{Track D
confirms this with an independent, non-Abelian gauge theory}: gauge eliminated analytically, no penalty
term, and Q-MAML wins decisively, again matching Track A rather than Track B. \\
\midrule
The zero-parameter fixed point is not a simple Pauli-sum-vs.-Hermitian-matrix property, and is narrower
than first thought. It holds exactly on four Pauli-sum Hamiltonians (Heisenberg, molecule, Schwinger,
$\mathbb{Z}_2$ LGT, SU(2) LGT) but not on two Hermitian-matrix ones (scalar field, mostly not on SUSY QM),
consistent with a Pauli-sum link. \textbf{Track E breaks that link}: its Hamiltonian is a Pauli sum yet
the fixed point does not hold, the only such case. Leading (untested) hypothesis: every fixed-point
Hamiltonian has a purely-$Z$ mass/diagonal term, while Track E's mixes $Z$ and $X$. \\
\midrule
No classical scheme is a reliable default across tracks ($\pi$: best/2nd on A/B, worst on C/F). Q-MAML is
the only consistently competitive scheme, under interpolation only. Track C's phase-stratified test shows
this does not extend to extrapolation across a phase transition, and in one direction Q-MAML starts out
worse than task-independent baselines. \\
\midrule
\textbf{Track F: a physics-derived initial state is not automatically better than a generic random one.}
The source paper's own hand-picked basis-state init is the worst-starting, close-to-worst-finishing scheme
on SUSY QM, despite being chosen for its ground-state overlap: fidelity at $t{=}0$ is not the same as a
good landscape position for gradient descent given enough adaptation budget. \\
\midrule
\textbf{Track G confirms Track D's confirmation}: a third gauge theory (non-Abelian, un-reduced) with
gauge structure eliminated analytically again favors Q-MAML, though more modestly than A or D, and its
Zero-init exact fixed point (despite being a dense, non-Pauli-sum Hamiltonian) adds a third distinct
Zero-init outcome among Hermitian-matrix Hamiltonians (none/drift/exact on C/F/G), further undermining any
clean Pauli-sum-vs.-Hermitian-matrix rule for that pattern. \\
\midrule
\textbf{Not every genuine improvement shows up as a final-value win.} Track H (UCCSD, particle-number-
conserving by construction) is the first track where every scheme converges to the identical final energy;
Q-MAML's advantage is entirely in convergence \emph{shape} (a smooth descent vs.\ a shared oscillatory
transient every classical scheme goes through). Track I similarly has Q-MAML tie, not beat, Zero-init, with
a clean mechanistic reason (the task range's ground states are near-vacuum, and Zero-init already starts
there). Both are real, positive results for Q-MAML that a same-final-energy-only reading would miss. \\
\midrule
\textbf{Track J is a genuine null result, kept rather than dropped.} At $J{=}3/2$ (2 qubits), no
initialization scheme is distinguishable from any other, plausibly because the Hilbert space is too small
for a bad initialization to get stuck in. Reported as-is: a benchmark can be too easy to be informative,
and that is itself worth knowing before scaling a comparison up. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Pilot-scale: 2--8 qubits (Tracks D/E additionally verified and run at one larger qubit count each, single
seed), $n{=}3$-seed replicated throughout, hardware-efficient ansatz throughout except Track H (UCCSD). See
\S\ref{sec:limitations}.

Two further cross-track findings emerge only once multiple seeds are in hand. \textbf{The single-seed
``modest win'' framing was fragile}: Tracks F and G both looked like clean (if smaller) Q-MAML wins at one
seed, and both resolve into statistical ties with Uniform/Gaussian once replicated -- the single-seed
ranking was noise, not signal, in exactly the two tracks where the margin was already the thinnest. The
tracks with the largest single-seed margins (A, D, I, J) all replicate their qualitative finding cleanly;
the one with a still-open convergence question (E) is the one where multi-seed replication complicates
rather than confirms the picture. \textbf{Scaling the qubit count, not just the seed count, is where new
qualitative behavior shows up}: Track D's $10^3\times$ machine-precision margin over every classical scheme
at $N{=}2$ does not carry over. At $N{=}3$ (once the budget converges) $\pi$ is best at every seed and Q-MAML a stable second, and at $N{=}4$
(where schemes do converge) Q-MAML becomes seed-unstable, from $2.9\%$ (best) to $450\%$ (catastrophic)
across three seeds, while $\pi$ is the reliable scheme at $3.5$--$6.0\%$ -- while Track E's already-clean
win at $N{=}4$ gets \emph{more} decisive at $N{=}6$ (single seed there). The same ``more qubits''
intervention gives two tracks different outcomes, so no single scaling story covers this paper's
Hamiltonians, and Track D's own outcome is not what its $N{=}2$ result alone would have predicted.

\textbf{Why does the inner-loop-free algorithm beat the GSoC baseline (inner loop + stop-gradient)?}
Original hypothesis: the stop-gradient trick gives a weaker training signal.

Tested directly with an ablation sweeping the inner-loop step count from 0 to 20 (Table~\ref{tab:ablation},
GSoC baseline otherwise unchanged): final meta-loss is flat across the sweep and validation accuracy
\emph{increases} with more inner steps, opposite of the hypothesis. Gradient norm scales up with more inner
steps and tracks better, not worse, accuracy. Hypothesis retracted. Also, the GSoC baseline at zero inner
steps does not match Q-MAML's own numbers (meta-loss $0.68$ vs.\ $0.364$) even accounting for the known
support/query evaluation-split difference between the two implementations: why Q-MAML beats the GSoC
baseline remains open.

\textbf{Does the classification advantage shrink with qubit count, as \S\ref{sec:bp-classification}
predicts?} Partially. A 2-seed pass showed a reversal by 8 qubits; at 4 seeds it does not hold
(\S\ref{sec:classification}, Table~\ref{tab:correction-gap}): the gap trends smaller, directionally
consistent, but not confirmed. Solid at every qubit count: the win vs.\ Gaussian and the GSoC baseline.

\textbf{``Moderate gradient norm.''} The paper frames moderate norm as good conditioning. Q-MAML has the
\emph{highest} norm and the best accuracy: the opposite of a monotonic ``moderate is best'' rule. Does not
contradict the underlying point (avoiding vanishing gradients), but ``moderate'' should not be read as
``closer to some middle value is always better.''

differences), but produced exactly-zero gradients everywhere, not the theorem's predicted non-vanishing
ones. Documented separately, omitted from the main results.}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{\S\ref{sec:genuine-hep} is now $n{=}3$-seed replicated for every one of the ten tracks.}
    Two of the replicated tracks (F, G) turned out not to hold up as clean wins once seeded three ways --
    see their subsections and the cross-track synthesis above -- a reminder that this priority was
    correctly identified, not a formality. Track H (nuclear EFT, UCCSD) was the slowest to replicate by a
    wide margin: its per-step adaptation cost is markedly higher than the hardware-efficient-ansatz tracks,
    and one of its three seeds alone took $\sim$10.6h to pretrain (see Track H's own subsection).
    \item \textbf{Track B}: the $V$-sweep refuted the penalty-dominance hypothesis rather than confirming
    it. \textbf{The structurally gauge-invariant ansatz comparison (the track's originally intended fix) is
    no longer untried}: it works, and cleanly (\S\ref{sec:track-b}), confirming the mechanism directly, and
    is now $n{=}3$-seed replicated (Q-MAML best at every seed, $3\times$--$10\times$ ahead of the best
    classical scheme per seed). Still untried: annealing $V$ during pretraining; separating
    penalty/physical energy into adaptively-weighted loss terms; running the gauge-invariant ansatz at
    Table~\ref{tab:z2lgt-headline}'s full budget (the replicated result uses the reduced 4-task/200-iteration
    budget) and at larger system sizes.
    \item \textbf{Track A}: depth re-tune at $L{=}3$ is two spot-checks (depth 6, 9), not a scan. The
    $L{=}4$ failure is now resolved by a four-config scan (lower pretrain learning rate plus depth 18:
    Q-MAML $2.43\%$ vs.\ $\pi$ $8.65\%$), but that scan is single-seed, uses a reduced protocol (3
    held-out tasks, 150 iterations), and is a slice rather than a full grid: depth 18 was not run at the
    original learning rate, and depths above 18 or beyond $L{=}4$ were not tried. A full
    depth$\times$pretrain-budget$\times$qubit-count scan, with seeds, remains open.
    \item \textbf{Track C}: phase-stratified test is now $n{=}3$-seed replicated (correctly aggregated, after
    fixing a real bug in an earlier executed pass -- see \S\ref{sec:track-c}), confirming both directions;
    seed noise does not reorder either direction's ranking.
    \item \textbf{Track D}: does not reproduce the source paper's qubit-reduction trick or hadron-mass-ratio
    calculation -- the un-reduced Hamiltonian used throughout (including the $N{=}3$/$N{=}4$ extensions) is
    not the paper's own reduced circuit at their baryon scale. Now $n{=}3$-seed replicated at all of
    $N{=}2$ (robustly clean), $N{=}3$ (depth 4: no scheme converges at the carried-over 250-iteration
    budget, so the comparison is uninformative there) and $N{=}4$ (Q-MAML seed-unstable, $2.9\%$ to
    $450\%$; $\pi$ robust at $3.5$--$6.0\%$). Follow-ups: a converging $N{=}3$ budget (depth 6, $n{=}3$
    seeds) does not restore a Q-MAML margin ($\pi$ best at every seed, Q-MAML $5.7\times$--$8.9\times$
    behind), and a lower pretrain learning rate with more epochs does not fix the bad $N{=}4$ seeds
    (Q-MAML $312/2.9/450\%$, matching the original to four digits at two of three seeds). Still undiagnosed:
    why Q-MAML's $N{=}4$ seeds diverge, and why they are insensitive to the pretraining setting
    (pretraining diagnostics were not recorded for these runs). The un-reduced-Hamiltonian caveat means none of this
    settles what happens with the paper's own reduction trick.
    \item \textbf{Track E}: the entire premise is a reformulation (ground state instead of the cited
    literature's real-time dynamics), see \S\ref{sec:track-e}. Now $n{=}3$-seed replicated at $N{=}4$
    (a 2-of-3-seed win, not a clean one) and single-seed at a new $N{=}6$ extension (clean win); none of the
    five schemes had converged within the $N{=}4$ iteration budget, so the final-value gap may still narrow
    with more iterations, as it did in \S\ref{sec:vqe-heisenberg} -- still untested directly.
    \item \textbf{Track F}: now $n{=}3$-seed replicated (resolves into a statistical tie between Q-MAML,
    Uniform, and Gaussian rather than a clean Q-MAML win). \textbf{The unbalanced-split concern is now also
    directly tested}: a stratified (3/3/3) single-seed rerun reverses the original top two (Gaussian edges
    out Q-MAML) rather than merely narrowing the gap -- the same top-cluster-tie conclusion as multi-seed
    replication, reached a different way. Still uses a fixed-depth hardware-efficient ansatz, not the source
    paper's adaptive-VQE operator-selection approach; the stratified split is single-seed, not yet
    replicated at $n{=}3$.
    \item \textbf{Track G}: does not reproduce the source paper's qubit-reduced (matter-removal) Hamiltonian
    or its trapped-ion qudit gate set, since several of that reduction's intermediate equations could not
    be extracted with full confidence; uses the original, un-reduced Hamiltonian at $N{=}2$ instead. Now
    $n{=}3$-seed replicated (resolves into a statistical tie with Uniform rather than a clean Q-MAML win);
    no plaquette term exists in a 1D chain at any $N$.
    \item \textbf{Track H}: $N{=}2$ for all three nuclei. Now $n{=}3$-seed replicated (the identical-final-
    value finding holds to 4-5 significant figures at every seed, the tightest replication of any track
    here), task parameters $(t,C,D)$ swept continuously rather than fixed to the source paper's own
    experimentally-calibrated values. The \texttt{warm\_start\_nearby} baseline approximates, not
    reproduces, the source paper's own warm-start protocol.
    \item \textbf{Track I}: now $n{=}3$-seed replicated (the Q-MAML/Zero tie is robust at every seed); task
    range deliberately narrowed to the boson truncation's accurate regime, meaning this specifically tests
    the near-vacuum corner of the model's parameter space, plausibly why Zero-init is competitive here
    unlike elsewhere in this paper.
    \item \textbf{Track J}: now $n{=}3$-seed replicated (the null result is robust, not a single-seed
    artifact) at both $J{=}3/2$ and a new $J{=}3.5$ extension -- \textbf{the larger-$J$ fix was tried and
    did not work}: doubling the Hilbert space still shows no scheme separation, revising the diagnosis from
    ``too small to matter'' to genuinely open. Even larger $J$ and any comparison against HL-VQE's own
    reported numbers remain untried.
    \item \textbf{Ansatz}: nine of the ten tracks use the same hardware-efficient ansatz, not the
    symmetry-preserving circuits available for several of them (A/B/D/G); Track H uses UCCSD instead,
    the paper's only ansatz-family comparison. Isolates init-scheme comparison from ansatz-design
    confounds on nine tracks, but results there are about relative init quality on a fixed ansatz, not
    best achievable VQE performance.
    \item \textbf{CPU-only simulation} caps qubit count/depth (14 for the cheap VQE barren-plateau scan, 8
    for classification) below where plateaus are most severe on real hardware or GPU-scale simulation.
    \item \textbf{4 seeds} (HIGGS classification) resolves significance vs.\ Gaussian/GSoC baseline and
    catches the 2-seed false positive, but not the vs.-best-classical comparison ($p=0.08$--$0.29$). 8--10
    seeds is the next step. \textbf{Quark-gluon classification is 2 seeds only} (\S\ref{sec:classification}):
    the $\sim$18-point accuracy gap is directionally consistent with HIGGS but not independently
    significance-tested.
    \item \textbf{Reconstructed Heisenberg ansatz} (paper's appendix circuit unavailable): the molecule study
    (paper's exact ansatz) is the stronger evidence.
    \item \textbf{Task-generation}: single-variable quantile binning untested for sensitivity to bin count or
    binning variable.
    \item \textbf{Barren-plateau diagnostic scope}: the single-reference-parameter-gradient scan (standard
    McClean protocol) is now complemented by a quantum-Fisher-information/effective-dimension probe
    (\S\ref{sec:bp-vqe}), but only on the VQE-domain Heisenberg XYZ ansatz, qubits 4--8 (vs.\ the gradient
    scan's 4--14), 8 samples/point (vs.\ 80). Extending it to the full qubit range, to every Hamiltonian
    track, and to the classification domain is still open.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

We tested whether Q-MAML~\citep{leeqmaml2025} improves PQC trainability in a setting it was never evaluated
on (HEP classification), after finding neither prior GSoC attempt actually ran the published algorithm. Our
system matches the paper's own VQE results (reconstructed and exact-ansatz settings) before extending to
classification, where it beats classical init and the prior codebase with statistical significance at every
qubit count tested. A barren-plateau scan bounds the claim: the benefit is landscape placement, not plateau
resistance, and classification plateaus faster than the VQE setting the paper was validated on.

Beyond classification, a pilot on ten lattice/field-theory (and adjacent) Hamiltonians
(\S\ref{sec:genuine-hep}), what the GSoC 2026 proposal asks for, gives a mostly-positive, diagnosed result,
now $n{=}3$-seed replicated for \textbf{every one of the ten tracks}: robust wins on four (Schwinger;
$\phi^4$ scalar field, interpolation only; SU(2) LGT at $N{=}2$; collective neutrino oscillations,
decisively at $N{=}6$ though only 2-of-3 seeds at $N{=}4$), statistical ties for best on four (SUSY QM and
$\mathbb{D}_8$ LGT, both revised down from single-seed ``wins'' once replicated; light nuclei, the
strongest replication in this paper -- every scheme's final energy agrees to 4-5 significant figures at
every seed -- with Q-MAML faster; Yukawa, Q-MAML ties Zero-init at the precision floor), a genuine null
result on one (LMG, too small a system to differentiate any scheme, replication-confirmed), and
worst-scheme on one ($\mathbb{Z}_2$ LGT on its headline hardware-efficient-ansatz protocol -- \textbf{but
not on a structurally gauge-invariant ansatz, where Q-MAML is the best scheme at all three seeds},
$3\times$--$10\times$ ahead of the best classical scheme per seed; a non-gauge-invariant control ansatz, on
which Q-MAML is only tied with Uniform/Gaussian, supports structural gauge invariance rather than ansatz
novelty as the operative variable, though the two ansatze are different circuits, not a minimal pair).
\textbf{That two single-seed ``wins'' (F, G) did not survive replication is itself a headline finding}, not
a footnote: it is direct evidence that this pilot's own top limitation (single-seed tracks) was the right
thing to flag, and that a thin single-seed margin is not trustworthy without seeds to back it. A second
qubit-count axis, tried on Tracks D and E, gives different outcomes on each: Track D's $10^3\times$ margin
at $N{=}2$ does not carry over -- at $N{=}3$ (at a converging budget) $\pi$ is best at every seed and Q-MAML a stable second, and at $N{=}4$
Q-MAML becomes seed-unstable ($2.9\%$ at its best seed, $450\%$ at its worst, $n{=}3$) while $\pi$ is
reliable at $3.5$--$6.0\%$ -- whereas Track E's already-clean $N{=}4$ win gets more decisive at $N{=}6$
(single seed) -- so scaling qubit count is not a uniform stress test in either direction. Track H is
additionally the first ansatz-family comparison in this paper on
its own headline protocol, and the first track where convergence \emph{shape} rather than final value
carries the whole result; Track B's alternative-ansatz result above is this paper's second ansatz-family
comparison, and the one that changes a track's qualitative verdict rather than just its convergence shape.
Tracks E and I required reformulating their cited literature's question from real-time dynamics to a
ground-state search, stated explicitly rather than glossed over. Findings that
revise earlier sections: the zero-parameter fixed point is not universal and, per Track E, is not simply a
Pauli-sum-vs.-Hermitian-matrix property either; no classical init is a safe default across Hamiltonian
families the way Q-MAML nearly is, under interpolation, not extrapolation; even Q-MAML's own margin is
seed-, scale-, and ansatz-dependent rather than fixed, and can lose its lead along any of those three axes.
Which structural property of a Hamiltonian predicts whether learned initialization helps is now a concrete
question with four seed-robust wins, four seed-robust ties, one seed-robust null result, one diagnosed loss
that a change of ansatz resolves at three seeds, a qubit-count extension where the win gives way to seed
instability (D) and one where it strengthens (E, single seed), and several partly-tested mechanisms, rather
than an open guess.

pretraining diagnostics; test the same-basin reading); Track E at $N{=}6$ is single-seed; the gauge-invariant Track B result is at the reduced budget only; more seeds (8-10)
for both classification datasets; Track D at the source paper's own qubit-reduced,
hadron-mass-ratio $N{=}4$ circuit (not yet attempted, vs.\ this paper's un-reduced $N{=}4$); Track J beyond
$J{=}3.5$ (tried once, did not separate schemes -- an even larger $J$ or a different explanation is needed);
the untried Track G/H structural follow-ups noted in \S\ref{sec:limitations}; before submission.}
